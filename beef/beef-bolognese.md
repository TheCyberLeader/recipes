# Beef Bolognese

**Serves:** 6 | **Prep Time:** 20 min | **Cook Time:** 1.5 hr

## Description
A rich, slow-simmered Italian meat sauce with ground beef, soffritto, tomatoes, and a splash of wine. Best made in a big batch and served over tagliatelle or rigatoni.

## Ingredients

- 1.5 pounds ground beef (80/20)
- 12 ounces tagliatelle or rigatoni
- 1 medium onion, finely diced
- 2 celery stalks, finely diced
- 2 medium carrots, finely diced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/2 cup dry red wine
- 1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1/2 cup whole milk
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon dried thyme
- 1 bay leaf
- Fresh Parmesan, grated (for serving)
- Fresh basil or parsley (for garnish)

## Instructions

1. Heat olive oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the diced onion, celery, and carrot (soffritto). Cook for 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and the onion is translucent.

2. Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute until fragrant.

3. Increase heat to medium-high. Add the ground beef and cook, breaking it up with a spoon, until browned and no pink remains — about 8 minutes. Drain excess fat if needed.

4. Pour in the red wine and scrape up any browned bits from the bottom. Simmer for 3-4 minutes until the wine is mostly absorbed.

5. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 2 minutes until it darkens slightly.

6. Add the crushed tomatoes, milk, thyme, bay leaf, salt, and pepper. Stir to combine. Bring to a gentle simmer.

7. Reduce heat to low and simmer uncovered for 1 to 1.5 hours, stirring occasionally, until the sauce is thick and rich. Add a splash of water if it gets too thick. Remove the bay leaf. Taste and adjust seasoning.

8. Cook pasta in salted boiling water until al dente. Reserve some pasta water. Drain and toss with the Bolognese, adding pasta water as needed to loosen the sauce.

9. Serve topped with freshly grated Parmesan and fresh herbs.

## Notes

- The long, slow simmer is non-negotiable — this is what gives Bolognese its depth
- Milk softens the acidity of the tomatoes and tenderizes the meat
- Use 80/20 ground beef — leaner beef won't develop the same richness
- Bolognese freezes beautifully; make a double batch and freeze in portions
